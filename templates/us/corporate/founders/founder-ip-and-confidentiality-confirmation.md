This Confirmation is delivered by {founder_name} (the "Founder") to {company_legal_name}, a Delaware corporation (the "Company"), in connection with the end of the Founder's service to the Company, effective {separation_date} (the "Separation Date").

## Confirmations

### Survival of the CIIAA

The Founder confirms that the CIIAA — the {ciiaa_reference} between the Founder and the Company — remains valid, binding, and in full force and effect notwithstanding the end of the Founder's service on the Separation Date. In particular, the Founder confirms that the confidentiality, non-disclosure, and invention-assignment obligations set forth in the CIIAA continue to apply according to their terms following the Separation Date, and that the Founder will continue to comply with them. This confirmation restates and acknowledges obligations that already exist under the CIIAA; it does not modify, expand, or extend them, except as stated under Protected Disclosures.

### Return of Company Property and Access

The Founder confirms that, on or before the Separation Date (or on such later date as the Company and the Founder have agreed in writing), the Founder has returned to the Company, or has permanently deleted or relinquished, all Company property and materials in the Founder's possession or control, including devices, documents, records, and files, together with all Confidential Information as defined in the CIIAA. The Founder further confirms that the Founder has relinquished access to, and will not further access, the Company's systems, code repositories, email and messaging accounts, cloud drives, and other accounts and services, except to the limited extent, if any, that the Company authorizes in writing. The Founder may retain copies of records the Founder is required to retain by law or that relate solely to the Founder's own compensation, equity, or tax matters.

### No New Covenants; No Release of Surviving Obligations

This Confirmation creates no new obligation, restraint, or covenant of any kind. Nothing in this Confirmation imposes on the Founder any non-competition, non-solicitation, or confidentiality obligation beyond the obligations already existing under the CIIAA.

Nothing in this Confirmation, and nothing in any release, separation agreement, or settlement the Founder and the Company may enter into, is intended to waive, release, discharge, or otherwise limit the surviving confidentiality and invention-assignment obligations of the CIIAA confirmed above, all of which remain in full force and effect.

### Protected Disclosures

Nothing in this Confirmation, and nothing in the CIIAA obligations it confirms, restricts the Founder from reporting possible violations of law to a government agency or attorney, making disclosures protected by whistleblower laws, or discussing wages, hours, or working conditions as protected by law. The Founder does not need the Company's prior approval to make those disclosures or to notify the Company that they were made. To the extent the CIIAA could be read to restrict those disclosures or that activity, the Company and the Founder agree that it does not.

Pursuant to the Defend Trade Secrets Act (18 U.S.C. § 1833(b)), the Founder will not be held criminally or civilly liable under federal or state trade secret law for a disclosure of a trade secret that is made (a) in confidence to a federal, state, or local government official, either directly or indirectly, or to an attorney, solely for the purpose of reporting or investigating a suspected violation of law, or (b) in a complaint or other document filed in a lawsuit or other proceeding, if that filing is made under seal. The Founder may also disclose a trade secret to the Founder's attorney and use it in a lawsuit against the Company for retaliation for reporting a suspected violation of law, if any filing containing the trade secret is made under seal and the trade secret is not otherwise disclosed except by court order.
